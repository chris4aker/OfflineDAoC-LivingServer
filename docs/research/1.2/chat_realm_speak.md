# Chat and realm-speak: how players talked in early-2000s MMOs and in DAoC (2001-2004)

Scope note: about 19 tool calls. Primary-source coverage is strongest for one quantitative CMC
study (Peña & Hancock 2006), DAoC's channel set and etiquette (Camelot Herald guide and wiki), and
one dated 2002 FreddysHouse thread (EU realm stereotypes and writing style). Several named
academic sources (Herring, Ducheneaut et al., Yee, Steinkuehler, Nardi, Taylor, Wadley) were only
confirmed by title. Their contents are **not** verified here, so they go under Gaps. Lines marked
"[unverified, from background knowledge]" are leads to check, not findings.

## 1. Research on MMO and virtual-world chat: quantitative patterns

### Takeaway
The best quantified period study (Peña & Hancock 2006, a 2002 game) finds recreational game chat is about **77% socioemotional and 23% task**. Positive social talk outnumbers negative roughly 3:1. Experienced players use more conventions (emoticons, emotes, abbreviations), with about half their messages containing one. This fits the blind-test result: bots that talk only about tasks or lore, in tidy prose, stand out.

### Cited Findings
- **Corpus:** Peña & Hancock studied *Jedi Knight II: Jedi Outcast* (released March 2002) on a busy clan server. They recorded 5,826 messages from 65 participants in six 1-hour sessions over 2 weeks. 4,402 messages came from players and 1,424 were system messages. — [Peña & Hancock, Communication Research 2006 (PDF)](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf); [SAGE DOI](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0093650205283103)
- **Task vs social:** the mean share was socioemotional 0.77 (SD 0.18) and task 0.23 (SD 0.18), p < .001. Within socioemotional talk, positive was 0.74 and negative 0.26, "almost three times" as frequent. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)
- **Messages per participant (mean, N=59):** positive socioemotional 37.2, task 16.0, negative socioemotional 14.3, summons/greetings/partings 3.6, repairs 1.0. The SDs are larger than the means, so output is very uneven: a few players talk a lot and many barely talk. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)
- **Content of task talk:** task messages were mostly commands and suggestions, with some information exchange. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)
- **Experience effects:** the most experienced players were more positive (.84 vs .71 for low experience) and less negative (.16 vs .29). Their use of conventions rose to about half of all messages (t(58)=3.33, p<.001). Examples include "good fight", "LOL", the emoticon ":-)", and scripted emotes. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)
- **No message-length statistics** are reported in Peña & Hancock. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)
- **Iorio (City of Heroes, published 2010; later than the era):** orthographic variation (-ing/-in, -s/-z, place abbreviations such as "AP", conative abbreviations such as "lft") is predicted by message length, the standardness of nearby text, cognitive load, proximity in the virtual space, **how public the message is**, experience, avatar gender and social group. Spelling style is socially meaningful, not random noise. — [Iorio, UT Austin 2010](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/handle/2152/ETD-UT-2010-05-727)
- **Ducheneaut, Moore & Nickell, "Virtual Third Places":** a CSCW journal paper from 2007 (vol. 16, pp. 129-166) based on months of ethnography plus automated chat logging in *Star Wars Galaxies*. Its numbers were not retrieved. — [EUSSET record](https://dl.eusset.eu/items/f01f68ba-a365-40de-bd86-046477557db5/full)
- **Ducheneaut & Moore, "The social side of gaming" (CSCW 2004, SWG interaction patterns):** existence confirmed only. — [bibtex record](https://bibtex.github.io/CSCW-2004-DucheneautM.html)
- **"Alone Together" (Ducheneaut, Yee, Nickell & Moore, CHI 2006, WoW):** existence confirmed only. — [typeset copy](https://typeset.io/pdf/alone-together-exploring-the-social-dynamics-of-massively-2199ib5pkn.pdf); [SRI](https://www.sri.com/publication/fcd-publications/collective-solitude-and-social-networks-in-world-of-warcraft/)

### Inferences
- A bot population should aim for roughly **3 social lines to 1 task line** in casual public and guild contexts. Most social lines should be short and positive (grats, lol, ty, gj, gf). Few lines should be descriptive or lore-reflective. The lore musings the judge flagged ("bind stone and i are on first name terms") belong to no category Peña & Hancock coded heavily. They read as authored prose.
- Talk volume should follow a **heavy-tailed distribution across bots**: a few chatterboxes, many near-silent bots. This matches the blind-test finding that silence read as more human.
- Convention density (lowercase, abbreviations, emoticons, missing punctuation) should **rise with the bot's level or experience and with group or guild intimacy**. Text in public channels can be a little more standard (Iorio's "publicness" factor). Tidy capitals and full stops are a tell, especially from a veteran persona.
- Greetings and partings are a small share of lines (about 3.6 per person over hours) but they are ritual. A bot that never says "hi all", "gn", "cya" or "back" in guild chat misses a cheap human signal.

### Gaps
- No period numbers were found for message length, response latency, response rate to questions, or burstiness. Peña & Hancock lack length data. [unverified, from background knowledge] Herring-tradition chat CMC studies generally report very short median messages (a few words), disrupted adjacency (replies separated from their prompts by other lines) and parallel overlapping threads. A BZFlag study often attributed to Herring and colleagues, on gender and game chat around 2009-2010, was not located.
- Not retrieved: Ducheneaut & Moore's SWG cantina figures (e.g. how many players in a social space ever speak, the share of /emote use), the "Alone Together" WoW figures, Nick Yee's Daedalus survey data, Steinkuehler (Lineage, 2004-2006), Nardi (WoW, 2010; outside the era), T.L. Taylor *Play Between Worlds* (EverQuest, 2006), and Wadley (voice vs text). [unverified] Daedalus has articles on in-game relationships and on players' use of real-world language. The Daedalus archive is at nickyee.com/daedalus, e.g. [vol 7-1 index](https://nickyee.com/daedalus/archives/001313.php).

## 2. DAoC specifics: channels, etiquette, realm culture, slang

### Takeaway
DAoC's chat has distinct channels, each with its own job: /say local, /send tells, /g group, /gu guild, /o officer, /as alliance, /broadcast zone-local in towns and RvR keeps, /bg and /bc battlegroup, /chat chat groups of up to 200, /advice, and /em emotes. Period etiquette rested on terse readiness and OOM calls, directional "inc" calls in RvR, and norms against kill-stealing, begging and all caps. A dated September 2002 forum thread shows the realm stereotypes, the abbreviations and the lowercase, typo-laden style.

### Cited Findings
**Channels**
- **Commands and ranges:**
  - /s or /say reaches the immediate vicinity.
  - /send, /reply or R sends a private message within your realm.
  - /g reaches the group wherever members are.
  - /gu reaches online guild members.
  - /o is officer chat.
  - /as is alliance chat, with access set by guild leaders.
  - /broadcast reaches a zone or local area, but only in some places: towns such as Mularn and Howth, Prydwen Keep, and New Frontiers keeps and towers.
  - /bg join plus /bc is battlegroup chat.
  - /chat and /cg invite form chat groups of up to 200 players, with no xp or loot shared.
  - /advice lists player Advisors, who are not Mythic staff.
  - /em shows an emote.
  - /friend tracks logins, but /anon players are hidden.
  - The client has "thirteen chat/message channels".
  - — [Camelot Herald wiki: Chat](https://camelotherald.fandom.com/wiki/Chat)
- **Official beginner etiquette** (Camelot Herald "Beginner's Guide: The Social Side", undated, official site):
  - Ready checks use "gtg", "rfp" or "r".
  - Announce mana needs with "I need to med" or "OOM".
  - In RvR, follow the leader's commands and call enemies by direction, e.g. "inc" or "behind".
  - Don't attack mobs someone else is fighting (kill-stealing). This covers healing or debuffing them too.
  - Ask before buffing strangers.
  - All caps reads as yelling.
  - Ask whether someone is busy before making a request.
  - Don't beg strangers for gold, gear or buffs, unless someone has offered free buffs.
  - Find groups through LFG, the CGF tool, region chat or manual invites.
  - "OOC" is glossed as "Out of Character" when roleplaying.
  - — [Camelot Herald: Beginner's Guide, The Social Side](https://camelotherald.com/node/254)
- The same guide mentions **"region chat"** as a way to find groups. — [Camelot Herald](https://camelotherald.com/node/254)

**Servers**
- Current official server types are Ywain1-10 (clustered RvR, "Realms cannot communicate with each other"), Gaheris (cooperative PvE, all realms together, no RvR) and Pendragon (test). This is the modern lineup, not the 2001-2004 one. — [darkageofcamelot.com: RvR & Server Types](https://darkageofcamelot.com/content/rvr-server-types)
- **RP server evidence:** the blog's author (a Goodreads-listed author; name not checked) was "Roleplaying Team Lead for the Nimue server" about 20 years before a March 2022 post, i.e. around 2002. The post has no detail on RP norms. — [Goodreads author blog, 19 Mar 2022](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/22352499-twenty-years-ago-i-played-dark-age-of-camelot-i-was-the-roleplaying-te)

**Realm stereotypes and writing style (FreddysHouse, 17-18 Sep 2002, EU/GOA servers Excalibur and Prydwen)**
- Server and realm shorthand: "Excali", "Pryd", "Alb", "Mid", "Hib", and pairs like "Hib/Pryd". — [FreddysHouse "Return to Camelot"](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)
- **Albion:** biggest and bickering. Quotes: "too many peeps already ... always bytching" and "avoid Albion on Excalibur like the plague". — [FreddysHouse 2002](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)
- **Hibernia:** organised, friendly, overpowered. Quotes: "The greenpeace of DAoC (lol)" and "hibs are nicely overpowered really". — [FreddysHouse 2002](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)
- **Midgard:** elitist. Quotes: "'eliltists' who think they are gods gift to DAoC" and "Midgard on excalibur is full of crap and lamers". — [FreddysHouse 2002](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)
- **Slang:** "xping", "grps", "lists" (group sign-up lists), "uber buffs", "buff-bots", "lowbies", "mana bomb", "nerfing", "kill-stealers", "leechers", "solo like crazy".
- **RvR opinions:** "the trend is using only big forces" and "for true RvR dont even bother below 35".
- **Insults:** "lamers", "kiddies", "d00ds", "lewt dewds", "l33t speakers", "twits and gits".
- **Banter:** "who wants to be an Alb?" and "who wants to be a Mid? ;)". Mockery of "'I got killed by a purple while I was xping...' - sob threads". — [FreddysHouse 2002](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)
- **Writing style:** mostly casual lowercase, frequent typos ("sice", "peeps", "Mercanary"), emoticons ":) ;) :p", "lol", even "/cheer" typed into a forum post, and heavy abbreviation ("imo"). The original poster writes formally with British spelling, which shows a spread of registers. This is forum text, not in-game chat. — [FreddysHouse 2002](https://forums.freddyshouse.com/threads/return-to-camelot.98800)

**Talk as social fuel**
- A retrospective (Eurogamer, via GameBanshee, 6 Feb 2020; later material) says "Talk did a lot in Dark Age of Camelot". Rumours and legends of named players spread by word of mouth ("They say he's nearly level 50 - already!"). Long camps with the same group gave time to chat, and players had to "jostle for positions" at spawns. — [GameBanshee / Eurogamer retrospective, 2020](https://gamebanshee.com/3u3uf)

**Slang definitions** (a 2007 snapshot, slightly after the era)
- ding = level up; OOM = out of mana; LFG = looking for group plus class; AFK; KS = kill steal; twink; gank; zerg = overwhelming numbers, from StarCraft; "res" = resurrect. — [List of online-gaming slang, Wikipedia Feb 2007 static copy](https://wikipedia2007.classicistranieri.com/l/i/s/List_of_online-gaming_slang.html)

### Inferences
- **Channel choice carries meaning.** Bots should route lines by function:
  - group: ready, OOM, mez and pull calls, "inc", "rez pls"
  - guild: greetings, dings, grats, banter, gossip, rumours about named players
  - tells: buff, trade and help requests and offers
  - /say: short and mostly near camps or towns
  - /broadcast: town and keep calls only
  - /em: RP and silliness
- **Realm in-jokes and rivalry are period-accurate.** Each realm thought the other two were elitist, whiny or overpowered. Short sarcastic jabs ("hibs op", "albs whining again", "who wants to be a mid ;)") fit. Albs, mids and hibs are the plural forms. On a clustered server, realms can't talk to each other, so rivalry is talked about, not exchanged.
- **Buffbots** (second-account buffers) were a known, disliked topic by 2002. A bot complaining about them, or asking a guildie for buffs by tell, is authentic.
- **Rumour and reputation talk** ("u see that skald on the bridge again?") is a strong human signal that bots lack. Named enemy players and guilds were the period's gossip.
- **Low-capital, typo-tolerant style** should be the default, with a minority of "formal" writers (older, British or RP-minded players). Uniform style across the population is a tell.
- [unverified, from background knowledge; verify before relying] Common period RvR call forms: "inc", "INC!!", "inc mg" (milegate), "inc 5 from W", "inc alb zerg at amg", "keep under siege", "relic raid", "rp", "pbae" (point-blank AE), "mezzed", "purge", "assist X", "/stick", "stuck", "lag", "LD", "brb", "afk bio", "grats", "gz", "ding 32", "gg", "lfg 24 ench", "need rez". Officially /lfg and /trade are not on the wiki channel list. The 2001-2004 public chat for LFG may have been the region or zone channel the official guide calls "region chat".

### Gaps
- No primary source was found for the exact syntax of period keep and relic calls, mez and assist conventions, or the "/stick" and assist-train culture. Search the Camelot Herald archives, the Vault Network DAoC boards (via archive.org) and old guild RvR guides.
- The 2001-2004 roleplay server list (US: Guinevere and Nimue are believed RP-designated; EU: Avalon?) and RP norms (IC in /say, OOC in parentheses ((like this)), /em use) could not be confirmed from a source. The Nimue RP team-lead post confirms only that RP organisation existed around 2002.
- Class jokes were not sourced, e.g. "bard bots", "infiltrator gank", "zerk", "minstrel", "theurgist pets", "bonedancer", "cleric rez bot".
- Not researched: non-English EU servers (GOA's German and French servers, e.g. German "Midgard" community slang such as "inc" used inside German sentences). Code-switching evidence is a gap.
- The /lfg and /trade channels' existence and dates in the era are unconfirmed.

### Takeaway
No public, research-grade DAoC chat corpus was found. The usable material is scattered: dated forum threads (FreddysHouse 2002 is accessible), screenshots, retrospectives, and academic corpora from neighbouring games (JK2 2002, SWG, CoH).

### Cited Findings

- Peña & Hancock's JK2 corpus (5,826 messages, 2002) is the closest dated quantitative chat corpus from the era. Its raw logs are not public. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)

- The Penny Arcade forum's "do you remember Dark Age of Camelot" thread exists but now redirects. It can't be fetched directly; try archive.org. — [Penny Arcade (redirects)](https://forums.penny-arcade.com/discussion/14433/do-you-remember-dark-age-of-camelot)

## 4. What period and modern bots got wrong

### Takeaway
No source on period bot chat (Quake III bot chat, UO and EQ macro bots) was retrieved. The project's own blind test (2026-11-01, from the assignment) is the main evidence. It agrees with the literature above: humans are mostly short, positive, social, reactive and uneven in output, while the bots were generic, tidy, lore-heavy and unresponsive.

### Cited Findings
- Period norms that bots break, from the official etiquette guide: unasked buffing of strangers, begging strangers, and all-caps "yelling". Bots should break these norms only occasionally, and in character. — [Camelot Herald](https://camelotherald.com/node/254)
- Experienced players lean positive and rich in conventions; novices lean negative. A bot persona's tone should follow its level and experience. — [Peña & Hancock 2006](https://socialmedialab.sites.stanford.edu/sites/g/files/sbiybj22976/files/media/file/pena-cr-an-analysis.pdf)

### Inferences
Each blind-test failure (from the assignment) is mapped to its evidence-based fix.
- **Lore musings:** they don't appear in the empirical categories. Replace them with banter, gossip and grats.
- **Generic filler** ("good question", "happy to help"): this is assistant register, unlike the period's "np", "ty", "sure 1 sec" and "omw".
- **Tidy capitals and full stops:** go against the casual lowercase and typo style evidenced in 2002 forum text.
- **Near-duplicates:** humans vary, and convention use differs by person. Add per-bot idiolects: a fixed set of favourite tokens, typo habits and emoticon choice.
- **Ignoring RP, banter, offers and RvR calls:** humans answer "inc" with movement and "ok", "omw" or "where"; grats with "ty"; an offer with "ty!" or "sure". Rumours get "lol" or "that guy is a menace".
- **Replies that ignore what was said:** reply less often, but on topic. Silence is a valid human move.
- [unverified, from background knowledge] Quake III Arena bots (1999) used canned taunt and chat files that triggered on events (kills, deaths). Players recognised the repeating lines quickly. EQ and UO macro or "AFK" bots were spotted by not responding to tells, which led to the common "bot check" of sending a tell and watching for a reply.

### Gaps
- No primary sources on Q3 bot chat design (e.g. id Software or Jan Paul van Waveren's bot docs), on how players detected macro bots in UO, EQ or DAoC, or on modern LLM-NPC detection studies were retrieved.
