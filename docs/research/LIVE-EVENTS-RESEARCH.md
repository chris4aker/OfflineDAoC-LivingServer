# Visible stakes win, broken towns lose

The live events players still retell had four things in common. The world changed visibly. A shared goal pulled rival groups together or into open competition. The stakes were real and the rewards scarce. And the outcome was something players could actually change. WoW's Gates of Ahn'Qiraj war effort, Asheron's Call's Shard of the Herald defense, EverQuest's Sleeper kill, City of Heroes' Rikti beta invasion and DAoC's own keep-gated Darkness Falls all fit that pattern. The flops failed the same few ways, again and again. WoW's 2008 zombie plague killed the vendors and flight masters players depended on, with no way out. AQ, Rift's River of Souls and Throne and Liberty's Queen Bellandir all stuttered or crashed at the climax. EverQuest despawned a player-earned kill, and New World made defending worse than not defending. Events that ran on timers until they became chores, or that played themselves, wore out (BfA Warfronts, Warhammer's Public Quests, CoH invasions). Single-player and sandbox games add a solo-specific lesson. Events should hurt without destroying the player's own work, fire where the player is, and come with frequency controls, because every long-lived sandbox ended up shipping those controls. For Living Server 1.2, the evidence points to four event types. The first is a telegraphed, per-realm town or border invasion that fails into a temporary, recoverable state. The second is a director-paced roaming threat or world boss. The third is an AQ-style realm war effort tied to keep ownership. The fourth is a set of mirrored holiday festivals. All four should cap how many bots converge, keep the human decisive, and record outcomes in the player's save. Lag at the climax is the risk most likely to ruin the best moment, so it needs the most engineering attention.

## What this report covers and where the evidence is thin

The synthesis draws on five note sets. The first covers classic MMOs from 1997 to 2008: Ultima Online, Asheron's Call, EverQuest, vanilla WoW, City of Heroes, Lineage II, Star Wars Galaxies, RuneScape and DAoC. The second covers modern MMOs from 2008 to 2026: Warhammer Online, Rift, Guild Wars 2, WoW from Legion to The War Within, FFXIV, ESO, New World, Throne and Liberty, Lost Ark, OSRS and SWTOR. The third covers single-player, co-op and sandbox games: Terraria, Valheim, 7 Days to Die, Minecraft, RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, Skyrim, Conan Exiles, Sea of Thieves, Diablo IV, Destiny 2 and Fallout 76. The fourth covers DAoC official and freeshard events plus prior art on bot-populated servers: the AzerothCore city-siege module, the cMaNGOS playerbots, SPP Classics, 2009scape and Stanford's Generative Agents. The fifth gathers developer postmortems from Blizzard's engineers, Trion, ArenaNet, Jagex, Raph Koster and Valve's Left 4 Dead lineage.

The gaps matter for this project. No primary source turned up for Mythic-era (2001–2008) GM-run DAoC events, for the Catacombs or Atlantis launch events, or for the event calendars of Uthgard, Phoenix, Atlas, Blackthorn or OpenDAoC's public server. No reception data (forum or Reddit sentiment) was found for Broadsword's Doppelganger, Breach or holiday events. The research found **no quantitative retention or participation data** for any event in any game, so every hit-or-flop judgement rests on press retrospectives, developer statements and player commentary. EverQuest holidays, Anarchy Online's Alien Invasion, Mount & Blade, Stardew Valley, Animal Crossing, Path of Exile, Black Desert, and offline MMO adaptations (FFXI Trust, Dragon Quest X Offline) were not covered. Two engineering references could not be verified: Guild Wars 2's exact participant-scaling formula and the phase names and durations of Left 4 Dead's Director. Most importantly, **no source addresses whether a crowd of bots at an event feels alive or hollow to a human player**. That is the central open question for this server, and only playtesting can answer it.

## Hits changed the world, united rivals and let players decide the ending

The archetypal hit is WoW's 2006 Ahn'Qiraj war effort. Each faction had to hand in **just over a million gathered or crafted supplies**, while a separate quest chain built the Scepter of the Shifting Sands. Some scepter quests announced raid-boss fights to the whole server so rivals could interfere. Ringing the gong broadcast the ringer's name to every player and released a battle that lasted about ten hours. The ringer earned the Scarab Lord title, which only a few hundred players ever held. Destiny 2 director Luke Smith called the experience "completely transcendent" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/destiny-2-game-director-luke-smith-talks-about-wows-most-ambitious-world-event-ever)). The ingredients are worth naming: a visible server-wide progress goal, a named protagonist moment, a permanent world change (the gates stayed open), and allied NPCs such as the Cenarion Hold defenders and Saurfang fighting beside players. Blizzard's own engineers later said the crashes and chaos are part of why it is remembered. When they rebuilt it for Classic, **over 4,000 players became Scarab Lords** ([Blizzard Engineer's Workshop](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23504702/engineers-workshop-recreating-the-ahnqiraj-war-effort)).

Player-determined outcomes produced the most durable legends. On most Asheron's Call servers the Shard of the Herald was destroyed on day one. On Thistledown, defenders sacrificed themselves to it instead, and because AC monsters leveled by killing players, the shard kept growing stronger. A food-and-supplies economy formed around the standoff. Turbine made the defense canonical and erected a statue to the defenders ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2018/01/30/re-examining-the-significance-of-asherons-calls-shard-of-the-herald-event-on-its-first-deathiversary/)). On EverQuest's Rallos Zek, three rival guilds allied to kill the "unkillable" Sleeper. **Nearly 200 players** killed it in about four hours, and 400+ players congratulated the wizard who landed the killing blow ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-surprising-and-allegedly-impossible-death-of-everqu-1785741600)). City of Heroes' end-of-beta Rikti invasion, with ships filling the sky and developer-led bosses, became the benchmark later events "never matched." A 2008 column credited its huge visible changes to familiar scenery, fast large-scale action and a live event crew ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2008-09-23-the-daily-grind-what-makes-for-the-best-in-game-events.html)).

Two of the most beloved events were accidents, which says something about emergence. Corrupted Blood (2005) escaped Zul'Gurub on hunter pets and lasted about 26 days. Priests set up clinics, griefers spread the plague deliberately, and cities emptied. Jeff Kaplan now calls it an "epic" bug, and epidemiologists published on it in *The Lancet Infectious Diseases* ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/there-was-just-a-panic-players-scientists-and-blizzard-look-back-on-world-of-warcrafts-corrupted-blood-plague); [ISPR](https://ispr.info/2020/03/19/what-a-wow-virtual-outbreak-taught-us-about-how-humans-behave-in-epidemics/)). Raph Koster's UO stories make the same point from the developer side. Players stole invulnerable Santas' clothes to create "homicidal Santa Clauses," and when a guild barricaded a city gate with furniture, the team patched in furniture-chopping rather than deleting it. Koster calls this "improv drama," and says the best live stories are the ones players remember years later ([Raph Koster](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=36933)).

Recurring formats earned loyalty rather than legend. Lineage II's fortnightly two-hour castle sieges were the only window in which a castle could change hands, and players remember the voice-chat cheering and the prize of owning a fortress ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Lineage_II/Castle_Sieges); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/5-things-we-loved-about-lineage-2/castle-sieges-and-open-raiding/zc2df)). WoW's 2006 Scourge Invasion put purple skulls on the world map and used a **server-wide battle counter that unlocked vendors at 50, 100 and 150 wins**. City mobs stayed passive unless provoked, so players could ignore it ([warcraft.wiki.gg](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Scourge_Invasion)). Guild Wars 2's Twisted Marionette split a server into simultaneous lanes and is called "one of ArenaNet's best encounters" and the template for seven years of GW2 design ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/guild-wars-2s-best-boss-battle-returns-next-week-after-a-7-year-absence)). OSRS's Guardians of the Rift is praised for its "predictable, yet fluid play pattern" and rewards that stay useful outside the minigame ([devtrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/osrs/p/6676bc8a-guardians-of-the-rift-is-proof-that-the-osrs-dev-team-understands-what-makes-for-a-good-minigame)). In solo games, Diablo IV's Season 4 made Helltides available from the start and denser, and players called it the "best season start so far" ([Dexerto](https://dexerto.com/diablo/diablo-4-season-4s-revamped-helltides-make-launch-the-best-season-start-so-far-2723281)). Terraria chains its invasions into world change. Beating the Goblin Army unlocks the Goblin Tinkerer NPC, and the Frost Legion lets Santa move in ([Terraria Wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Events)).

DAoC's own best "event" was systemic rather than scripted. Darkness Falls opened only to the realm holding the most keeps, and players inside when control flipped could stay until they died or left, so enemy realms met inside. It was "widely praised" as one of the game's "crown jewels" ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2016/07/02/the-game-archaeologist-dark-age-of-camelot/)). Mythic reused the idea in Warhammer Online ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010-08-03-the-game-archaeologist-and-the-quest-for-camelot-the-history.html)). An outcome of the perpetual realm war changing world access is the most native template this project has.

## Flops broke services, crashed at the climax or took the ending away

The sharpest grievance was losing services with no escape. WoW's 2008 zombie plague escalated daily. The incubation timer shrank from five minutes to one, Argent Healers dwindled, and zombie raids targeted the NPCs players needed for reagents, battlegrounds, flights and auctions. Players couldn't fly yet, so they were trapped, and the plague clashed with Hallow's End ([Engadget WoW Archivist](https://www.engadget.com/2014-11-05-wow-archivist-the-zombie-plague-event.html)). Mike Schramm pinned down the difference from earlier events: those could be avoided by staying out of certain zones, while this one reached casual and low-level players who "just don't care" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2008-10-27-the-scourge-of-the-scourge.html)). Players called it "a contradiction to the very notion of PvE servers" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2008/10/25/player-reactions-to-wow-zombie-plague-are-both-positive-and-nega/)). The same event was also among WoW's most publicized and is credited with feeding the subscription peak ([Engadget WoW Archivist](https://www.engadget.com/2014-11-05-wow-archivist-the-zombie-plague-event.html)), so disruption cuts both ways. The sources disagree on how it ended. Blizzplanet reports Blizzard saying it ended on schedule ([Blizzplanet](https://www.blizzplanet.com/blog/comments/zombie_invasion_world_event_ends)), while Blizzard's 2022 Classic post says it "was ended significantly earlier than originally intended" ([Icy Veins](https://icy-veins.com/forums/topic/68245-zombie-plague-and-scourge-invasion-fixes-and-clarifications)).

Technical collapse at the payoff moment recurs more than any other failure. Luke Smith remembers AQ's "profoundly sad framerate" at the gong ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/destiny-2-game-director-luke-smith-talks-about-wows-most-ambitious-world-event-ever)). A Tichondrius diary records repeated crashes before the event and bugged Colossi wiping raids ([Lurker Lounge](https://lurkerlounge.com/forums/thread-4804.html)). Blizzard's engineers quantified the problem. The packets generated by one action grow with the square of the player count, from **380 at 20 players to 2,248,500 at 1,500**, and faster hardware could not fix it ([Blizzard Engineer's Workshop](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23504702/engineers-workshop-recreating-the-ahnqiraj-war-effort)). Rift's Scott Hartsman said "the ultra-scale issue happens when you end up with a thousand people standing in the same ten-foot square." He called super-dense battles "really not fun" and named space and time compression "bad ideas, just don't do them" ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/rifts-scott-hartsman-responds-how-he-plans-to-makr-live-world-events-fun-for-everyone)). Throne and Liberty's Queen Bellandir drew crowds that caused disconnects and lost weapon drops ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/throne-and-liberty/queen-bellandir)). A single scripted Christmas tree deed in UO "brought down the entire service on Christmas morning" ([Raph Koster](https://www.raphkoster.com/?p=36933)). The sandbox and emulator evidence matters most for a bot server. 7 Days to Die players report that more than about 30 zombies "hit the CPU... really hard" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/251570/discussions/0/3148556875515786607)), and SPP Classics, which runs 1,000 random bots by default, warns of lag after 30+ minutes and suggests lowering bot counts ([SPP Classics](https://github.com/celguar/spp-classics-cmangos)).

Taking away an earned outcome, or rewarding only one path, poisoned otherwise great events. EverQuest staff despawned the Sleeper at about 26% health after three hours. Players "felt robbed and were outraged," and SOE apologized and respawned it ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-surprising-and-allegedly-impossible-death-of-everqu-1785741600)). Turbine later admitted that the Shard gave no reward for choosing not to kill ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2018/01/30/re-examining-the-significance-of-asherons-calls-shard-of-the-herald-event-on-its-first-deathiversary/)). New World inverted the incentive. Failing an invasion downgraded a town's crafting stations, more so the further defenders had got, so without about 50 well-geared players not defending was the rational choice ([AltChar](https://www.altchar.com/game-news/invasion-defence-in-new-world-is-counter-productive-as-intended-apparently-aO7bA4U52vmI)).

Events also died from being too easy, too frequent or too obligatory. Within two months of launch, Warhammer's Public Quests were "ghost towns" farmed for first-stage influence ([Ten Ton Hammer](https://www.tentonhammer.com/articles/war-public-quests-hey-where-is-everybody)). Producer Jeff Hickman said the PvE was too easy, so players had little reason to group ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2009-09-17-agdc09-jeff-hickman-speaks-on-wars-shortcomings.html)). BfA Warfronts offered "war on a scale unlike anything I've seen in WoW," but needed "no need to strategize". One reviewer went AFK for ten minutes and the battle still ran smoothly ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/how-is-world-of-warcraft-battle-for-azeroth-holding-up-one-month-after-launch)). That is the closest live-MMO analogue to a bot army winning while the human watches. City of Heroes players described "event fatigue" from frequent invasions and proposed having them fire far less often ([CoH forum archive](https://forumarchive.cityofheroes.dev/topic/242750)). UO's Treasures of Tokuno reprise angered first-run players whose stockpiles lost value ([UOGuide](https://www.uoguide.com/Treasures_of_Tokuno)). DAoC's Trials of Atlantis is the domestic warning. Artifacts and Master Levels were seen as necessary for RvR, so players shelved the RvR they loved to grind PvE, and the expansion was "reviled" ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2016/07/02/the-game-archaeologist-dark-age-of-camelot/)).

Solo and sandbox games failed in their own way: unreliability, and hitting what the player came to do. Conan Exiles' Purge spawned mobs inside walls, sometimes never fired despite a full meter, and led "many players" to disable it ([Funcom forums](https://forums.funcom.com/t/am-i-missing-something-or-is-the-purge-system-rubbish/299821)). Valheim's raids were described as likely the biggest barrier to enjoying it as a relaxed building game. Its pure 20%-per-46-minute roll produced droughts of 70 to 117 days for some players and four troll raids in a row for another ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/valheims-difficulty-settings-will-let-you-disable-the-games-most-annoying-feature-or-make-it-worse); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/0/3073117690261865225/?ctp=2)).

## Telegraphs and schedules decide who shows up, and quiet makes the peaks land

Visible in-world signals drew the broadest participation, and hidden lore drew confusion. Asheron's Call delivered its story through NPC delivery quests and translated documents, and "many players missed" what it meant ([Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/2018/01/30/re-examining-the-significance-of-asherons-calls-shard-of-the-herald-event-on-its-first-deathiversary/)). Rift's first six months show the fix. Trion changed the world map so players "could see where the action was taking place," broadcast zone events in-game, buffed defensive abilities so lightly populated zones could push back, and gave invaders a growing debuff the longer they held a hub. Invasions remained "a major part of the game" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-10-12-enter-at-your-own-rift-a-six-month-retrospective.html)). ArenaNet's Colin Johanson wanted events seen, not read about: "hundreds of lions running all over every street of the city," rather than a "little black box with white text" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010/05/19/guild-wars-2-lead-content-designer-expands-on-dynamic-events-sys/)).

The best telegraphs escalate across hours and through several channels. In 7 Days to Die, the HUD day number turns red at 08:00. Thunder and a reddening sky follow at 18:00, the sky is very red by 21:00, and a directionless scream starts the horde at 22:00 ([7DTD Wiki](https://7daystodie.wiki.gg/wiki/Blood_Moon_Horde)). Sea of Thieves allows one world event per server at a time, each under a giant, distinct sky sign that players can sail toward or away from ([Sea of Thieves Wiki](https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Events)). ESO's Harrowstorms gather red mist and wipe out an NPC camp before they spawn ([UESP](https://content2.uesp.net/wiki/Online:Harrowstorms)). FFXIV's S-rank hunts announce themselves in zone chat with "You sense the presence of a powerful mark…" ([Console Games Wiki](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Hunt)). Terraria names the direction ("approaching from the west!"). Its Martian Madness can be prevented by killing the probe that spotted you ([Terraria Wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Events)). Minecraft 1.21 made raids opt-in: drinking an Ominous Bottle sets one up, and milk cancels it during a 30-second countdown ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/minecraft/raid-omen-trial-omen-minecraft-1-21-all-need-know)). The "two doors" pattern recurs across mature games: a rare natural trigger plus a summon item for the same event.

For players with limited time, predictable windows beat random short ones. One working-adult blogger playing twice a week overlapped a Legion Assault only once in two weeks and felt "like despite putting in the same time… I'm falling behind" ([Talarian](https://talarian.blogspot.com/2017/04/wow-invading-on-schedule.html)). Rift's one-hour world event left absent players out ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/rifts-scott-hartsman-responds-how-he-plans-to-makr-live-world-events-fun-for-everyone)). When Diablo IV hid its world-boss schedule, players reverse-engineered it to the millisecond and kept it in spreadsheets ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/players-claim-to-have-worked-out-the-hellish-details-underpinning-diablo-4s-timed-events)). Even with Dragonbane Keep's fixed two-hour loop and map warhorn, WoW players kept asking for in-game timers ([PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/world-of-warcraft/dragonbane-keep-timer)). Clockwork has its own cost, though. Critics said GW2's world came to feel "clockwork, more than dynamic" ([Kill Ten Rats](https://www.killtenrats.com/2014/03/13/gw2-transitory-content)).

Director systems resolve the tension between surprise and predictability by pacing on the player's state rather than the clock. Left 4 Dead's Director tracked a per-player "stress level" and added or removed threats accordingly, which Valve called "procedural narrative" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-discomfort-zone-the-hidden-potential-of-valve-s-ai-director)). Turtle Rock's Phil Robb put the principle plainly: "You can't appreciate the action unless you have some inaction" ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/82787/how-evolve-assures-action-peaks-and-valleys)). RimWorld's storytellers weigh wealth, colonists, recent deaths and time since the last major event. Its designer Tynan Sylvester states the goal as "The game tries to hurt but not kill the colony," with winning raiders who "steal or kidnap and leave, instead of killing everyone." He adds that "The richness comes when several events interact" ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-i-rimworld-i-fleshes-out-the-i-dwarf-fortress-i-formula)). Skyrim tied dragon frequency to main-quest progress, aiming for a rate that is "Not at a rate that is annoying... it still feels special" so that a dragon "changes whatever you're doing at the time" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/todd-howard-on-skyrims-toughest-monsters-the-more-of-the-main-quest-you-do-the-more-dragons-youll-run-into)).

## Participation rewards and per-head scaling work, but the crowd can erase the hero

Rewards that credited everyone outlasted competitive ones. GW2 paid XP, gold and karma specifically to avoid loot disputes ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2010/05/19/guild-wars-2-lead-content-designer-expands-on-dynamic-events-sys/)). Rift changed participation credit so it was "no longer based on how you do compared to others" after low-level players feared being shut out ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-04-06-enter-at-your-own-rift-evaluating-events.html)). FFXIV hunts reward party-combined enmity, which encourages grouping ([Console Games Wiki](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Hunt)). Throne and Liberty went the other way, ranking all participants and giving the best rewards to the top ten ([Games Lantern](https://throneandliberty.gameslantern.com/guide/dynamic-events-guide)). Jagex's Tempoross brief sets out the scaling philosophy most clearly. It should support "group sizes from 1 to 40+," with players "competing with the boss as opposed to each other!" It should not give "the best XP and best fish rates," so it doesn't drain other methods. Failure should be real but soft, through a forced retreat or a phase reset, and reward pools always contain something ([Tempoross Poll Blog](https://oldschool.runescape.wiki/w/Update:Fishing_Skill_Boss:_Tempoross_Poll_Blog)).

Trion also showed that difficulty should be tuned toward a target success rate. Hartsman contrasted an event that succeeds 80% of the time with one that succeeds 10%, "since that's what makes the game fun." The same talk found that one event had "200 soldiers and no leaders," because "strangers are scary," which public grouping fixed ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2011-10-13-gdc-online-2011-rifts-scott-hartsman-on-surviving-and-thriving.html)). Tequatl shows what happens when difficulty is fixed and the population can't meet it: "most players on most servers will never see a Teq kill," and random turret players could "do absolutely nothing" ([Kill Ten Rats](https://www.killtenrats.com/2013/09/23/gw2-the-ebb-and-flow-of-tequatl)). GW2's Living World critics described the opposite problem: "50 players in a blob is too many to feel like you matter" ([Kill Ten Rats](https://www.killtenrats.com/2014/03/13/gw2-transitory-content)). Sandbox games scale openly by head-count. Sea of Thieves' Kraken needs 2–3 tentacles for a sloop and 7–8 for a galleon ([Sea of Thieves Wiki](https://seaofthieves.wiki.gg/wiki/World_Events)), and 7DTD caps concurrent horde size at the lowest of three limits ([7DTD Wiki](https://7daystodie.wiki.gg/wiki/Blood_Moon_Horde)). RimWorld shows the risk of scaling on wealth. Its raid points rise with colony wealth, which spawned a whole "wealth management" playstyle of burning and hiding goods ([RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Wealth_management)). Its later "wealth-independent progress" mode is the designer's own correction ([RimWorld Wiki](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_storyteller)).

Time-limited exclusivity drew crowds and resentment in equal measure. GW2's two-week Living World episodes were "largely disliked," and later seasons were made permanent ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/guild-wars-2s-best-boss-battle-returns-next-week-after-a-7-year-absence)). Blizzard extended Wrath Classic's Plague Cure quests a week "to ensure that more players have an opportunity to participate" ([Icy Veins](https://icy-veins.com/forums/topic/68245-zombie-plague-and-scourge-invasion-fixes-and-clarifications)). OSRS's design director describes the opposite stance: "There's no benefit to logging in specifically today versus tomorrow. There's no fear of missing out" ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/mmo/how-did-the-most-timeless-mmo-hit-a-new-peak-of-240-000-players-12-years-after-launch-protecting-peoples-achievements-and-progression-while-preventing-fomo)). Scarcity still works for prestige. RuneScape's one-time 2001 Christmas crackers created the game's most valuable rares ([RuneScape Classic Wiki](https://classic.runescape.wiki/w/Christmas_cracker)), and Scarab Lord's rarity is part of its legend.

## DAoC and bot-server prior art already sketch a native template

Broadsword's modern events share one template, seen in at least six events. A themed threat appears in known RvR geography. A daily group quest pays a no-trade token, which mirrored per-realm NPCs exchange for RvR consumables, and zone bonuses run alongside. The 2022 Doppelganger Invasion attacked couriers and realm guards in the frontier mountains and mazes. It paid 10 Invader Seals per daily quest, capped at **300 per character**, no-trade, and wiped at event end ([Camelot Herald](https://camelotherald.com/article/doppelganger-invasion-event-and-bonuses)). By the 2024 anniversary article, the event had ended with Caer Benowyc, Bledmeer Faste and Dun Crauchon "returned to their normal state," which implies it had temporarily altered frontier keeps ([DAoC.com](https://www.darkageofcamelot.com/article/23rd-anniversary-celebrations)). The 2024 Breach had demons pushing "through the old maze ruins," with a mirrored quest NPC in each realm: Lady Idris, Yda Llonwy and Fathor ([Camelot Herald](https://www.camelotherald.com/frontpage)). Holidays follow the same mirrored structure. The Mythic-era Midwinter Festival gave each realm its own visitor (Santa Nicholas, Lucia of the Light, Jack Frost) plus the shared Olibar the Tomte ([GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/k4xy)). The 2023–24 version added frontier-keep gather quests, a Frozen Cavern instance with a Jack Frost boss, and daily gifts ([Camelot Herald](https://www.camelotherald.com/article/midwinter-festival-returns-0)). Shrouded Isles lore already supplies a mirrored invader for each realm: Drakoran allied with Morgan Le Fay ravaging Avalon, Morvalt savages besieging Aegir, and Fomorians invading Hy Brasil ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/dark-age-expansion-details/1100-2876575/)). One caution: no reception data was found for any Broadsword event, so the template is documented but its popularity is not.

Freeshards appear to be moving from GM one-offs to systemic RvR events. Eden's Season 3 (March 2025) lists weekly Campaigns, Realm Invasions "through the old frontiers," Frontlines, Dynamic Missions and War Supplies ([r/daoc mirror](https://axebps-redlib.hf.space/r/daoc/comments/1jizqmf/eden_s3_intro_and_overview/mjjv3b8/?context=3)). Phoenix players remember Darkness Falls feather farming as what "kept that feeling after I hit 50," and name the small-group-versus-zerg imbalance as what kills frontier fun ([r/daoc mirror](https://redlib.hyperreal.coffee/r/daoc/comments/14c0g7f/best_part_of_daoc_shards/joohk87/?context=3)).

The closest bot-server analogue is AzerothCore's mod-city-siege. It schedules sieges at a random 120–240 minutes apart, each lasting 30 minutes. A 150-second roleplay phase has attackers yell before the fight, and tiered attackers default to 15 minions, 5 elites, 2 mini-bosses and a leader against 10 defenders. It recruits only idle random bots, skipping any that are dead, in combat, grouped or inside instances, and returns them "to their original location and strategies afterward." Its README warns that it "PROBABLY WON'T WORK," and that hand-placed waypoints cause stuck units ([GitHub](https://github.com/DustinHendrickson/mod-city-siege)). cMaNGOS playerbots document quests, battlegrounds, guilds and RPG wandering but nothing on world events ([cMaNGOS docs](https://thoriumlxc.github.io/cmangos-tbc/bots/playerbots)). 2009scape disabled player botscripts on its live server, citing economic and community harm, but kept them as an option for "single-player users and self-hosters" ([2009scape](https://2009scape.org/updates/2021-10-18-Trick-or-Treat.html)). That is direct evidence that bots farming event rewards can inflate an economy. Stanford's Generative Agents offers the strongest case for seeding an event rather than scripting it. One agent's wish to throw a Valentine's party spread through 25 agents into invitations, dates and coordinated arrival without further scripting ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442)).

## Recommendations for Living Server 1.2 (inference, not research findings)

Everything in this section is the author's inference from the evidence above. Statements about the server's existing features come from the project context the owner supplied (bot-run keep and relic sieges, dragon raids, 200–300-bot Darkness Falls and Summoner's Hall expeditions, realm-wide announcements, a launcher Realm Events tab and records, PC-calendar holiday chatter, true rumours, lore talk and the Leeroy gag). They are not verified here. The server's distinguishing condition reshapes most of the research. Live MMOs struggled with empty events, absent leaders, time zones and griefers. Here the server controls head-count, bots can be made to lead, and there is only one schedule that matters: the owner's a few hours a week. The live-MMO failures that remain fully relevant are lag at the climax, broken services, the human being made irrelevant, rewards that inflate the economy, and fatigue.

### Four event types, in priority order

| Priority | Event type | Why it earns the slot | What it reuses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Telegraphed realm invasion** of a town, border keep or frontier hub, using a mirrored realm invader (Drakoran in Albion, Morvalt in Midgard, Fomorians in Hibernia) | Combines the most-proven format (Scourge Invasion, Rift zone events, Broadsword's template) with lore already in the game; fails into a recoverable state instead of a broken one; easy to mirror equally | Realm-wide announcements, keep siege behaviour, true rumours for scouting |
| 2 | **Director-paced roaming threat or world boss**: a dragon flight, a demon breach from the mazes, a named roaming warband | Cheapest to make frequent and varied (Skyrim's "changes whatever you're doing", FFXIV S-rank cues, ESO Harrowstorms); keeps a short session eventful without a calendar | Existing dragon raids, the Leeroy gag as an occasional variant |
| 3 | **Realm war effort chapter** in the AQ style: bots and the player contribute supplies, kills and keep captures toward a visible realm total, ending in a set-piece that unlocks something lasting | The strongest "remembered forever" pattern, and DAoC-native when tied to keep ownership the way the Darkness Falls gate is; bots are well suited to visible turn-in loops | Bot keep and relic sieges, DF and Summoner's Hall expeditions, Realm Events records |
| 4 | **Mirrored seasonal festivals** keyed to the PC calendar, with one visitor per realm, a cosmetic reward track and a light seasonal boss | Low risk and high charm; the owner's calendar already drives holiday chatter; returns every year so nothing is lost for good | Holiday chatter, lore talk |

The invasion comes first because it gives the best ratio of stakes to risk and fits existing systems most directly. Each realm gets its own lore invader on mirrored terrain, which meets the equal-realms rule by construction. A shared frontier threat (doppelgangers or maze demons) can occasionally draw all three realms' bots into one fight. Roaming threats come second because they solve the few-hours-a-week problem. A director can guarantee something happens during a session without a schedule the owner must catch. The war effort comes third because it is the most memorable and the most dangerous to build: it carries the AQ climax-lag risk and the ToA grind risk. It should be rare, perhaps one chapter every few weeks of the owner's play time. It should never be needed for RvR power. Festivals come fourth only because they are the least novel. They are also the safest to ship on by default.

### Design rules the evidence supports

**Telegraph across several channels and hours, then open with a horn.** A good sequence starts with bot scouts and true rumours, follows with a realm-wide broadcast naming the place and direction (as Terraria does), then a weather or sky change at the target, and finally a clear start signal. Upcoming events and their state should be exposed in-game: town criers, bot chatter, and a status command in the style of a `/events` command. Players who can't see a schedule they care about build their own trackers, as Diablo IV showed. Do this through server-side and in-game channels. If the existing Realm Events records can be written from the server without touching launcher code, feed them. Otherwise leave the launcher alone, so shadowofze's launcher behaviour stays unchanged.

**Key timing to the owner's session, not the wall clock.** Major events should start only while the human is logged in and should never resolve to failure offline. At most, the realm war should tick slowly in the background. Festivals should run for weeks, following Blizzard's own extension of the Plague Cure and GW2's move away from two-week windows. Every event type should also get a second way in, a "call it now" summon (Terraria, Minecraft), so the owner can play the event he wants on his schedule.

**Tune toward better-than-even odds, with real but soft failure.** Following Hartsman's success-rate tuning, aim for a success rate of roughly 60–75% when the human takes part. Failure should behave like Tempoross and RimWorld: a retreat, a phase reset or a robbery, never a wipe. A failed invasion can occupy the hub for a limited time, with Rift's growing occupation debuff on the occupiers. That leaves the town scarred and invites a counter-event, the way GW2's dredge fort does. Never repeat New World's mistake: a failed defense must never leave things worse than not defending.

**Never kill the services the player depends on.** Merchants, trainers, stablemasters, the Realm Exchange and selling points stay invulnerable or are visibly evacuated to a nearby fallback. This protects the owner's stablemaster-route and natural-break-selling rules, and avoids the 2008 zombie plague's core grievance. Any spreading or occupying mechanic needs a hard geographic and time bound, has to exclude pets and companions, and needs a kill switch. Corrupted Blood escaped through pets.

**Keep the human decisive inside the crowd.** Warfronts and Tequatl's useless turrets show the danger of a battle that plays itself. Give the human a named objective that bots defer to: carrying the banner, destroying the anchor crystal, or striking the final blow on a summoned champion. Bots should visibly do the optional "heroic" steps in the Destiny 2 manner, so the human learns them by watching. Bots should also self-organise in view of the human: forming and merging groups, calling targets, shouting in chat. That is the substitute for the human crowd that made AQ and the Sleeper feel social. The Generative Agents result suggests seeding intent and letting bots spread it, rather than teleporting bots in silently.

**Record outcomes, and reserve permanent change.** Small events reset. Mid-tier events leave marks that can be recovered from: ruins, a garrison captured or restored, a merchant who arrives once the town is saved. Rare war-effort finales change something for good and get a chronicle entry or a statue in the AC Thistledown tradition. All of it lives in the player's save, because release worlds start from `release-world-defaults.json`. A one-command restore to the default world state should accompany it.

**Keep rewards real but capped.** Pay normal real loot from event mobs. Add titles, trophies, cosmetics and mounts tied to the event, with capped no-trade tokens on the Doppelganger model. Event gear should never be best in slot, following Tempoross's "shouldn't give the best rates" and the ToA lesson. Apply the same caps to bots, or bot farming will inflate the coin and Realm Exchange economy, as 2009scape found. Bots that earn and display event titles provide social proof without FOMO.

**Mirror everything across realms and audit the balance.** Every invasion, festival visitor and war-effort chapter should have three equivalent versions on equivalent terrain, at equivalent difficulty and reward. Log success rates per realm so drift shows up. If one realm keeps winning, Rift's beta hubs falling and the Phoenix "zerg" complaint both suggest the director should give the weaker realm a counter-event rather than a buff to the winner.

### Settings: on by default, with the controls every lasting sandbox shipped

| Setting | Suggested default | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Live events master switch | On | Owner wants settings on by default; Valheim, RimWorld and 7DTD show players still expect an off switch |
| Event frequency | Default (also Off / Low / High) | Valheim's world-modifier slider; changeable mid-save with no penalty, as in RimWorld |
| Per-type toggles (invasion, roaming threat, war effort, festivals, Leeroy) | All on | The per-type control Valheim lacked and players modded in |
| Warning lead time | A few in-game minutes for small events, longer for majors | 7DTD's configurable warning hour |
| Max bots converging per event, and max near the human | Conservative, tuned by profiling | Lag control; mod-city-siege caps recruits by tier |
| Target difficulty or success band | "Better than even" | Trion's success-rate tuning |
| Offline resolution | Pause majors; slow tick for the war effort | Session-keyed timing for a player with a few hours a week |
| Summon or schedule now | Available | Terraria and Minecraft's opt-in second door |
| Safe zones | Starter towns and capitals' service cores exempt | Terraria and Valheim locality rules |

These could follow the existing settings pattern used by `classes / enable_sluaghbinder`, with storage in `offline_local_options` or `offline_population_settings` and goal weights in `bot-goals.json`. That is a placement suggestion and has not been checked against the code.

### Risks to plan for

Climax lag is the biggest risk. AQ, Rift, Throne and Liberty and the SPP and 7DTD reports all show it, and on this server a crash takes the whole world down with it. The defence is to recruit like mod-city-siege, taking only idle, ungrouped bots up to a cap. Stagger arrivals, park the excess bots on parallel content elsewhere (AQ gave low-level players other war work), and profile tick time with the actual N bots before release. The bot population is already the headless load-test client Blizzard had to build. Correctness follows closely behind. AQ Classic hit a turn-in race condition and lost progress on restart, and Wrath Classic's timers desynchronised. Event state belongs in the database, with idempotent counters and independent start and stop commands. Pathing is the next risk. mod-city-siege's stuck waypoints and Conan's spawns inside walls argue for validating march routes against existing navmeshes, with an off-screen spawn fallback. The project rules forbid rebuilding meshes globally to fix one route.

The remaining risks are design ones. The human may end up spectating a bot win. Fatigue can set in if the same invasion fires too often, which argues for CoH-style rarity, variety and a director's quiet stretches. Event grind can crowd out RvR, the ToA trap. Lore names can be wrong: the per-realm invader names above are sourced to GameSpot, but deeper NPC choices (Morgana's undead, jotuns, Siabra) were not verified. Finally, the AGENTS.md honesty rule applies to any finale set in Darkness Falls or at level 70+. Raid AI there should not be described as implemented beyond what has been checked in game.

## Conclusion

Mature online games learned to manage a scarce, unruly crowd. A bot server's problem is the reverse: its crowd is abundant and obedient. Almost every live-MMO failure that came from the crowd (empty events, no leaders, time zones, griefers, being shut out of loot) can be designed away here, and those fixes show up as strengths in the recommendations. What remains are the failures that came from the system itself: the server choking at its own climax, an event breaking the town it was meant to dramatize, and a battle that no longer needs the player. Those deserve the engineering budget.

The deeper implication is that the best event for one human among thousands of bots may not be a scripted event at all. It may be a seeded situation that the bots' own goals turn into a story, as Corrupted Blood, Thistledown and Smallville's party did. Scripted invasions, roaming threats, war efforts and festivals give that emergence a frame and a pace. The hard question the research could not answer, whether a crowd of bots at an event feels alive or hollow, can only be settled by the owner playing it. That makes per-realm success logging and a few honest playtest notes the most valuable output of 1.2's first month.
