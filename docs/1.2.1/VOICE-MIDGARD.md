# How Midgard's lore sounds (voice guide for the NPC pass)

## Rule 1: phenomenal and tight

Owner, 2026-11-22: "Stories should be phenomenal and tight", "don't be long for the sake of length".

- Every sentence adds a fact, an image or a consequence. A sentence that only restates, frames or announces goes.
- Nothing old is cut (decision 3). Tighten by rewording, never by dropping an event or a detail.
- A new name replaces a vague phrase; it does not sit beside it. "The skalds of Jordheim say" becomes "Sven of Jordheim
  sings", not "The skalds of Jordheim, Sven among them, say". A name should usually leave the sentence the same length or
  shorter.

## Who tells Midgard

Midgard is a realm of witnesses. Its tellers are skalds, slate-keepers, beam-cutters, guides and trappers, each of
whom stood somewhere and saw one thing: Orri on the cliff above the dig, Brisa at the beam, Hallveig at Trollheim's
gates, Thyri's uncle at the fork. The gods are alive and listening (Ragnarok has not come), so the realm speaks
of them as of neighbours who may overhear.

- **Diction:** short Germanic words; few Latin ones. Kennings sparingly and once ("hall under hall like a hand laid under
  a hand"). Norse names keep their sound (Hlidskialf, Svarhamr); the realm's own words are glossed once (Uppat, "the
  upward ones").
- **Rhythm:** saga plainness. Statements in order, no hedging adverbs, the consequence last and short. "He kept it. He
  kept it longer than any wolf should live." Repetition is a saga device: use it once a page, on purpose.
- **Forms:** the slate (Orri, Hrefna), the beam reading (Brisa), the skald's verse, the trap-line tally, the hearth tale
  told by a named teller, the roll of a warband ("it was eighty strong, and it had a skald with it").
- **Stance:** courage is assumed, so the teller notes the cost and moves on. A dry joke is allowed; despair is not.

## Model passages

1. `bosses/stanga`, story:
   > He kept it. He kept it longer than any wolf should live, through winters and plagues and the coming of the dwarves to
   > the wood above, and in the end he died at it, of the old one's rot, and she did not let him leave.

   Two words, then the life, then the sting. The repetition does work.
2. `bosses/grul-mgnuk`, book page:
   > From the slates of Orri of Hagall: I lay on the cliff above the dig a whole night once, to see what the callers did.

   A named witness who went and looked. Everything after it is believed because of the night on the cliff.
3. `locations/trollheim`, story:
   > Trollheim is the city of the Troll Fathers, cut down into the rock of Aegir under the Gripklosa Mountains, hall under
   > hall like a hand laid under a hand.

   One image carries the whole plan of the city.

## Passages to avoid

1. `bosses/geyra`, legend:
   > A small group of the strength the quest asks kills her easily, then it's back to Lynnleigh.

   A walkthrough step. In-world: Lynnleigh waits for the runner's message on Strider Island, and the runner does not
   arrive.
2. `locations/raumarik`, legend:
   > People go for their great quest: Loken in the east for healers and shamans, Oona at the river fork in the south for
   > hunters and shadowblades, ...

   A list of class assignments. Each line wants its quest giver and a reason (Inaksha's vision, Masrim's war).
